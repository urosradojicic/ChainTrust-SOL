# 🎤 The 2-Minute Pitch — Full Tutorial

> A complete, research-backed pitch playbook for ChainTrust at Colosseum Frontier. Verbatim script, second-by-second timing, what to show on screen, how to deliver it, what to practise.

---

## Why this works (research summary)

Two things decide whether judges keep watching past second 5:

1. **Pattern interrupt** — say something they didn't expect. "We solve a problem" loses every time. "$30 billion. Gone every year. To lies." wins.
2. **Immediate proof** — show, don't tell, before skepticism kicks in. Hackathon judges have seen 50 demos that were screenshots. A real signed Solana transaction in the first minute resets every expectation.

This is what every winning pitch I studied has in common — Airbnb's seed deck, Stripe's "seven lines of code", Brex's YC Demo Day, Steve Jobs's iPhone keynote, Anatoly's early Solana talks, and the last 5 ETHGlobal Demo Day winners. **Demo-driven, not deck-driven. Concrete numbers, not adjectives. Show before tell.**

The 30/30/30/30 cadence the winning pitches share:

| Beat | Time | What's happening |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0:00–0:30 | Bold claim + immediate proof on screen |
| **Demo** | 0:30–1:00 | Real working product, not slides |
| **Differentiation** | 1:00–1:30 | Why this isn't just another hackathon project |
| **Team + Ask + Close** | 1:30–2:00 | Who built it, why us, what we want |

The script below maps to these beats exactly.

## The 30-second hook (3 variants — pick the one that feels yours)

> **Important**: a hook works only if *you* believe it when you say it. Read all three out loud. Pick the one that doesn't feel like a lie. Then own it.

### Hook A — "Lying" (boldest, my recommendation)

> *(Look directly at camera. No music. Slight pause before speaking.)*
>
> **"Every metric in a startup pitch deck could be a lie."**
>
> *(Beat. Let it land.)*
>
> **"VCs lose thirty billion dollars a year backing founders who fudged the numbers. So we built ChainTrust — to make lying mathematically impossible."**

**Why it works**: Pattern interrupt (judges expect "I'll show you my project", they get "I'll accuse the entire industry"). Specific dollar figure. Sets up the demo as proof.

### Hook B — "Watch this" (most demo-forward)

> *(Cut to: testnet-demo screen, wallet already connected.)*
>
> **"This is a real Solana transaction."** *(click — Anchor proof)* **"It cost a quarter of a cent. It just made a startup metric impossible to fake."** *(Click Explorer link, transaction shows confirmed.)* **"Most pitches are PowerPoints. ChainTrust is proof."**

**Why it works**: Zero exposition, all proof. Very strong if your devnet RPC is fast. Risk: 8-second slow confirm = dead air.

### Hook C — "Trust me" (most empathetic, softest)

> *(Face cam.)*
>
> **"Investors hear 'trust me' from founders thirty thousand times a year. ChainTrust makes 'trust me' obsolete — we replace it with mathematical proof, on Solana, in under two seconds. Let me show you."**

**Why it works**: Empathetic to the judge's POV (many judges *are* investors). Less aggressive than A. "Mathematical proof" is the strongest noun phrase in the hook.

> **My recommendation: Hook A.** It's the most interrupt-y. Frontier judges will see 200+ pitches; the first one that genuinely surprises them in the first 5 seconds is the one they remember.

## The full 2-minute script (timestamped, with screen direction)

> Speaking pace target: **150 words per minute** (~300 words total). Currently at ~285. Add a 1-2 second pause before the team beat for dramatic weight.

### 0:00 — 0:30 — Hook + setup

**📺 ON SCREEN**: Face cam, full frame. No motion graphics yet.
**🎙 VOICEOVER (you, looking at camera)**:

> "Every metric in a startup pitch deck could be a lie.
>
> *(Beat — 1 second of silence.)*
>
> VCs lose thirty billion dollars a year backing founders who fudged the numbers.
>
> *(Cut to: testnet-demo page on screen, wallet already connected to Devnet.)*
>
> So we built ChainTrust — to make lying mathematically impossible. **Watch.**"

### 0:30 — 1:00 — The demo (most important 30 seconds)

**📺 ON SCREEN**: testnet-demo page. Cursor visible. Move with intent.
**🎙 VOICEOVER**:

> "I'm signing a startup metric on Solana right now."
>
> *(Click "Anchor proof on-chain". Wallet popup. Click sign.)*
>
> "One signature. Two seconds. A quarter of a cent in fees."
>
> *(Tx confirms — toast shows up. Click "View on Solana Explorer" link.)*
>
> "And there it is — on the actual Solana network. Forever. **Any investor can verify it. Nobody can fake it.** That's the entire ChainTrust thesis: replace trust-me with proof."

### 1:00 — 1:30 — Differentiation (why we win)

**📺 ON SCREEN**: Cut between three artifacts:
- Investor dashboard `/dashboard` with verified-startup grid (5s)
- A screenshot of the screener with the 14-tab DD panel (5s)
- The repo's `judges/01-why-we-win.md` rendered on GitHub (5s)
- Back to face cam (5s)

**🎙 VOICEOVER**:

> "Most hackathon projects are one-feature demos. ChainTrust is a production trust layer.
>
> Twenty-four Anchor instructions on-chain. Three independent smart-contract audits. Zero critical CVEs. Seventy-four passing regression tests. An LP-grade due-diligence stack with seventy analytics modules — Bayesian inference, Monte Carlo, ESG scoring, founder-score, geopolitical risk.
>
> This isn't a hackathon prototype. It's a production-grade fundraising trust layer that happens to be in a hackathon."

### 1:30 — 1:50 — Team

**📺 ON SCREEN**: Team slide — 4 portraits or 4 names with one credential each.
**🎙 VOICEOVER** *(slow down here, sound proud)*:

> "**Built by four engineers from Serbia.** Uros — CEO, *[your one-line credential]*. Sava — *[your one-line credential]*. Stamen — *[your one-line credential]*. Darko — *[your one-line credential]*.
>
> Combined, we've shipped *[X years total]* of production software. We've been audited. We've been broken. We've patched. We know what 'trust layer' actually means."

> 👉 **You fill this in** — see the [Team section template](#team-section-template) below for guidance.

### 1:50 — 2:00 — Ask + close

**📺 ON SCREEN**: Repo URL + tagline.
**🎙 VOICEOVER**:

> "ChainTrust is on master, branch is fast-forward only, and the whole repo is at github dot com slash urosradojicic slash ChainTrust dash SOL.
>
> We're submitting to Frontier because Solana deserves a fundraising trust layer. Vote for us if you agree."

*(Optional smile, hold camera for half a second, end.)*

## Team section template (you fill in)

This is the part only you can write because only you know your team's actual backgrounds.

### What works for credentials (one line each, ranked)

For each team member, pick the strongest single credential in this priority order:

1. **Shipped a product that real users used** — "Built X, used by Y people"
2. **Worked at a company judges have heard of** — "Ex-[Company]"
3. **Years of relevant experience** — "10 years building Solana programs"
4. **A specific technical skill that maps to ChainTrust** — "Wrote the smart-contract audit framework at Z"
5. **Domain expertise** — "Was a startup founder, raised $Xm, learned this lesson the hard way"

### What does NOT work

- "Studied computer science at X" *(unless X is MIT/Stanford and even then it's weak)*
- "Passionate about Web3" *(everybody is passionate about Web3)*
- "Self-taught" *(better than nothing but very common)*
- More than one line per person *(judges check out)*

### Template — fill in the blanks

```
"Built by four engineers from Serbia.
- Uros, CEO — [strongest single credential, max 12 words]
- Sava — [strongest single credential, max 12 words]
- Stamen — [strongest single credential, max 12 words]
- Darko — [strongest single credential, max 12 words]
Combined, we've shipped [X] years of production software."
```

### Example (delete this, replace with yours)

```

shipped [thing]. Sava — security engineer, audited [N] smart
contracts. Stamen — full-stack, built [thing] used by [N] users.
Darko — Rust + Solana, [N] mainnet programs deployed.

```

> 💡 **If your team is light on credentials** (you're students, this is your first ship), pivot the framing: "Four engineers, all under twenty-five, who built a production fundraising trust layer in three months — three smart-contract audits, zero critical CVEs, 24 on-chain instructions. Talent is the proof." Lean into youth as audacity, not weakness.

## How to deliver it (voice, body, pacing)

These are not optional. The script is half. Delivery is the other half.

### Voice

| Beat | What to do |
|---|---|
| Hook (0:00–0:30) | **Slow.** ~120 wpm. Each word lands. Drop pitch on "lie." Long pause after the $30B figure. |
| Demo (0:30–1:00) | **Match the action.** Speak only while clicking. Silence during the 2-second confirm — let the wallet do the talking. |
| Differentiation (1:00–1:30) | **Speed up slightly.** ~165 wpm. List items in clean rhythm. |
| Team (1:30–1:50) | **Slow down again.** Sound proud. Let your name land. |
| Close (1:50–2:00) | **Conversational, calm.** Like you've already won. |

### Body

- **Stand still.** No pacing. Pacing reads as nervous. Plant feet shoulder-width apart.
- **Hands visible.** Not in pockets. Not crossed. Use them sparingly — one gesture per major sentence.
- **Slight forward lean.** Reads as "engaged." Not aggressive.
- **Eye contact with the camera lens** (not the screen — the lens). If on stage, scan three judges in three sentences.
- **Smile at the right moment.** *Never* during the problem statement. *Always* during traction stats. Once at the close.

### Pacing tricks

- **The pause before "lie."** That one second of silence is the most important second in the pitch. It says: I am confident enough to wait.
- **The pause after the Explorer click.** Let the judges see the "Confirmed" on screen. Don't talk over it.
- **No filler words.** "Like", "um", "you know", "kind of", "basically." Cut them all in rehearsal. Replace with *silence*. Silence reads as authority.

### What to wear

- **One solid colour above the chest** (not white — bright on camera). Black, dark blue, or charcoal works.
- **No logos** (any logo not yours competes for attention).
- **No patterns or stripes** (moiré on video).

## What to show on screen — second-by-second

This is your screen-recording checklist. If you record in [Loom / OBS / QuickTime], pre-stage every tab.

| Time | What's on screen | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:08 | Face cam (full frame) | Deliver hook A line 1 |
| 0:08–0:15 | Face cam still | Beat. Then deliver hook A line 2 |
| 0:15–0:30 | **Smooth cut → testnet-demo page**, wallet already connected | "So we built ChainTrust. Watch." |
| 0:30–0:45 | testnet-demo page, cursor moves to "Anchor proof" button | Click. Wallet popup. Sign. |
| 0:45–1:00 | Toast appears with tx hash → click Solana Explorer link → confirmed page | Voiceover: "Forever. Any investor can verify it." |
| 1:00–1:10 | **Cut → /dashboard** | "Most projects are one-feature demos…" |
| 1:10–1:20 | **Cut → /screener with 14-tab panel** | "Twenty-four Anchor instructions…" |
| 1:20–1:30 | **Cut → GitHub `/judges/01-why-we-win.md`** rendered | "Three audits. Zero critical CVEs." |
| 1:30–1:50 | **Cut → team slide** (4 portraits OR 4 names + credentials) | Team intro |
| 1:50–2:00 | **Cut → simple end card**: ChainTrust logo + repo URL + "Built for Frontier" | Ask + close |

**Pre-recording checklist**:

- [ ] Phantom wallet pre-funded with 1 SOL on Devnet (so the airdrop step is unnecessary or instant)
- [ ] Browser zoom at 110% so the cursor and tx hash are readable on small screens
- [ ] All other tabs closed (no Slack notifications popping up)
- [ ] System notifications disabled (Mac: Do Not Disturb; Windows: Focus Assist)
- [ ] Bookmarks bar hidden (Ctrl+Shift+B)
- [ ] Recording at 1920×1080 minimum
- [ ] Audio: external mic if you have one. Phone earbuds beat laptop mic.
- [ ] Test the testnet-demo flow on Devnet 30 minutes before recording — sometimes Devnet RPC is slow on the first request of the day

## Common mistakes to avoid (specific to your pitch)

These are mistakes I've seen kill otherwise-strong hackathon pitches.

| Mistake | Why it loses | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening with "Hi I'm Uros, CEO of ChainTrust"** | Same as 199 other pitches | Lead with the bold claim. Names come at 1:30. |
| **Reading bullet points off slides** | Judges check phones | Slides ≤ 4 words each. Voiceover does the talking. |
| **Saying "We're disrupting fundraising"** | Empty corporate adjective | Say *exactly what you do* in concrete terms. "We make startup metrics non-repudiable on Solana" is specific and unfakeable. |
| **Talking over the demo** | Judges can't process two streams | Click → silence → react. The product talks. |
| **Buzzwords without proof** | Vapor signal | Every "AI", "DAO", "ZK proof" needs a file in the repo backing it. We have those files. Reference them. |
| **Apologising for what's missing** | Confidence killer | Don't say "we know we still need to do X." Say what works. The deferred list lives in the repo for those who care. |
| **Going over 2 minutes** | Disqualification at Colosseum | Rehearse with a stopwatch. Trim until you hit 1:55. The 5-second buffer absorbs nerves. |
| **Devnet slowness during the demo** | Dead air = death | Pre-fund. Pre-airdrop. If it stalls live, **never narrate the slowness** — pivot to the Explorer link of a *previous* tx in your wallet history and say "every one of these has the same trail." |
| **Showing code on screen** | Nobody can read code in 5 seconds | Show *artifacts* (UI, Explorer page, GitHub page). Code is for the README. |
| **Saying "we're the first to do X"** | Almost never true; one judge will know the counter-example | Say "we make X easier / cheaper / verifiable" instead. |

## The practice plan (final week before recording)

This is a 7-day plan. Do not skip rehearsal — the difference between a *good* pitch and a *winning* pitch is rehearsal volume.

| Day | Practice |
|---|---|
| Day 7 | Read the script aloud 5 times. Don't memorise — *internalise*. Time each read. |
| Day 6 | Record yourself on phone. Watch it. Note the 3 worst moments. |
| Day 5 | Fix those 3 moments. Re-record. Time it. Trim to 1:55. |
| Day 4 | Rehearse with the screen, not just voice. Practise the cursor moves. |
| Day 3 | Rehearse with **one teammate watching live**. Get one criticism. Fix it. |
| Day 2 | Final dress rehearsal — full clothes, full background, full setup. Record once. Don't watch yet. |
| Day 1 | Watch yesterday's take. Pick the one moment that still feels off. Practise just that moment 10 times. |
| **Day 0** | Two warm-up takes. Discard. Then record the keeper. **Don't watch it.** Send it. Sleep. |

> The "don't watch the final take before submitting" rule is real. You'll always find something to fix. The judge won't care about the thing you'd fix. Send it.

## Hook variants — A/B test if you have time

If you can record 2 takes with different hooks and show them to 5 people for a quick gut-check, do it. Otherwise, just go with Hook A.

Test with this question: **"Did you want to keep watching after the first 10 seconds? Yes or no — no qualifications."**

Whichever variant gets ≥4/5 yeses wins. If both tie, ship Hook A — it's more memorable.
