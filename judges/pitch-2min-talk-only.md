> No screen. No demo. No deck. Just you talking. ## Why a talk-only pitch is harder

The demo version cheats: when the wallet popup appears, the product does 30% of the persuasion work for you. Take that away and the words have to do everything.

Three things change:

1. **Pause more, not less.** Silence is the only visual you have. Use it as punctuation.
2. **Rule of three.** "Trust me / verify me / forever" lands harder than any single phrase. Listeners pattern-match in threes.
3. **Direct eye contact + forward lean.** You ARE the visual. Slouching in a no-demo pitch reads as "this person doesn't believe what they're saying."

The script below uses all three deliberately.

## The script (verbatim — ~265 words, ~1:50 with pauses)

> Read it out loud once before reading the delivery notes. The rhythm matters more than analyzing the rhythm.

### 0:00 — 0:25 · The hook (rule of three: trust / verify / forever)

> "Every founder you've ever funded told you **'trust me.'**
>
> *(Beat — one full second of silence.)*
>
> Some of them lied. Investors lose thirty billion dollars a year to that single broken sentence.
>
> ChainTrust replaces 'trust me' with **'verify me.'** On Solana. In two seconds. For a quarter of a cent. **Forever.**"

### 0:25 — 1:00 · The mechanism (specific, technical, audited)

> "Founders sign a SHA-256 hash of their metrics with their wallet. The hash anchors on a Solana program we built — twenty-four on-chain instructions, three independent smart-contract audits from OtterSec, Sec3 and CertiK, zero critical CVEs.
>
> Once it's on-chain, any investor with the URL can verify it on Solana Explorer. The metric is now mathematically attached to the founder forever. Not 'we promise.' **Cryptographic proof.**"

### 1:00 — 1:30 · The depth (why this is more than a single feature)

> "Behind that simple flow is a production-grade due-diligence stack. Bayesian inference for trust scoring. Monte Carlo for valuation scenarios. Founder-score, moat analysis, ESG taxonomy, geopolitical risk. Seventy analytics modules an LP would expect.
>
> Bloomberg-for-Solana-startups. We're not a screenshot of a screener. **We're the screener.**"

### 1:30 — 1:50 · Team

> "Four engineers from Serbia. Uros leads. Sava builds. Stamen ships. Darko audits.
>
> Combined, we've shipped *[X]* years of production software, been audited three times, broken twice, patched both. **We've earned the right to call this a trust layer.**"

> 👉 You fill in the credentials — see the [Team section template in `pitch-2min.md`](pitch-2min.md#team-section-template). The four-name beat fits in ~10 seconds; if it takes longer, you're listing too much.

### 1:50 — 2:00 · Ask + close

> "We're submitting to Frontier because **Solana deserves a fundraising trust layer.** The whole repo is open at *github dot com slash urosradojicic slash ChainTrust dash SOL.*
>
> Vote for us if you agree."

*(Optional: hold eye contact for half a second after the last word. Smile. End.)*

## Delivery — what's different from the demo version

### 🎙 Voice — pause more

The demo version has natural silence built in (the wallet popup, the confirmation). Talk-only has none of that. **You have to manufacture silence.** Three deliberate pauses:

1. **After "trust me."** Full second. The whole hook hinges on this pause.
2. **After "thirty billion dollars."** Half second. Lets the number land.
3. **Before the close.** Quarter second. Signals "I'm wrapping up — listen now."

If you don't pause, the rhythm is flat and the rule-of-three structure collapses into mush.

### 🧍 Body — you are the visual

| | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Eyes** | Direct eye contact with the listener (or camera). Hold for sentences, not seconds. | Look down at notes. Look at your hands. Glance to the side mid-sentence. |
| **Hands** | One open gesture per major sentence. "Trust me" → palm up. "Verify me" → palm forward. | Pocket your hands. Cross your arms. Fidget with anything. |
| **Posture** | Slight forward lean. Chin level. | Slouch. Lean back. Cross your legs (reads as defensive). |
| **Distance** | If sitting: edge of the chair. If standing: feet hip-width, weight evenly. | Lean against anything. Put your hands on a table to "anchor." |

### 🎯 Energy curve

```
HIGH ┤        ┌──┐                        ┌─┐
     │        │  │              ┌─────────┘ │
     │   ┌────┘  └──┐           │           │
LOW  ┤───┘          └───────────┘           └─
     0:00      0:25  0:35    1:00  1:30   1:50   2:00
       hook    pause  mech.   depth  team  close
```

- **Hook**: lift on "trust me", drop on "lied", lift on "verify me", land hard on "forever."
- **Mechanism**: steady, almost-flat. This is where you sound technical and unflappable.
- **Depth**: list rhythm — slight upward inflection on each item.
- **Team**: warm. The names are the only proper nouns in the pitch besides the company; let them land.
- **Close**: calm, certain. Like you've already won.

### 🚫 Three things that kill talk-only pitches

| Mistake | Why it loses |
|---|---|
| **Reading off notes** | Listener can hear it. The rhythm goes flat. Learn the script — don't memorise it word-for-word, internalise the beats and let the words come naturally each time. |
| **Speed-talking to fit 2 minutes** | Audible nervousness. Cut content instead of rushing it. |
| **No "trust me" pause** | The rule-of-three rhythm depends on it. Without that pause the hook is just a sentence. |

## How to practise this version specifically

The demo version benefits from screen rehearsal. The talk-only version benefits from **mirror rehearsal** and **phone-recording rehearsal**. Three drills:

### Drill 1 — The mirror

Stand in front of a mirror. Deliver the pitch making eye contact with yourself. Do this three times. Notice when your eyes drift. The third time, force them to stay locked.

### Drill 2 — The phone recording (audio only)

Record audio-only on your phone. Listen back **with your eyes closed**. If the rhythm of the rule-of-three doesn't land without visuals, the pitch fails. Re-record until the rhythm lands.

### Drill 3 — The 6-foot test

Stand in a room. Have someone (or your phone propped up) at the far end. Deliver the pitch loud enough to be clearly heard at 6 feet without shouting. Most people are *too quiet* in person — projecting at 6 feet calibrates volume for any room you're actually in.

## The 30-second cut (for elevators)

If you genuinely have only 30 seconds (a literal elevator, a quick intro at a party), use this:

> "Every founder tells investors 'trust me.' Some lie. Investors lose thirty billion a year to it. ChainTrust replaces 'trust me' with cryptographic proof on Solana — twenty-four on-chain instructions, three smart-contract audits, anchored in two seconds for a quarter of a cent. Open at github dot com slash urosradojicic slash ChainTrust dash SOL."

That's 65 words = ~25 seconds. Hands you a name they can find later, and lays the hook for them to ask "tell me more."

## When NOT to use this version

The talk-only pitch is the *backup*. Use the [demo-driven version](pitch-2min.md) by default whenever a screen is available — your working product is the strongest evidence you have. **Hiding the demo to seem confident reads as hiding the demo.**

Save this version for the contexts where there's genuinely no screen.
